# Binary CTA library

30 binary calls-to-action that work in B2B cold email. Every one of
these is yes/no answerable, which is the JMC standard.

Use these as inspiration, not verbatim — adapt the wording to the
recipient's signal + pain.

## Time-bound binary asks (highest-converting)

1. "Worth 15 minutes Thursday at 11am PT?"
2. "Open to a 10-minute walkthrough Tuesday?"
3. "Up for a 20-minute working session next week?"
4. "Can I grab 15 minutes on Friday?"
5. "Quick 10-minute call Thursday morning?"

## Resource-offer binary asks

6. "Want me to send the <peer> case study?"
7. "Should I send the 3-page write-up?"
8. "Interested in the <metric> teardown?"
9. "Want the 1-page summary?"
10. "Should I drop the comparison doc?"

## Intro / referral binary asks

11. "Should I introduce you to <name> who solved exactly this?"
12. "Want me to connect you with <peer>?"
13. "Interested in talking to <peer-team> who's 12 months ahead?"

## Specific-action binary asks

14. "Should I rerun this analysis with your data?"
15. "Want me to audit your <thing> and send the top 3 levers?"
16. "Should I send the framework template?"
17. "Want the scoring rubric?"

## Sequence-close binary asks

18. "If <pain> isn't on the roadmap right now, no worries — should I follow up in Q4?"
19. "Should I keep these coming, or close the loop?"
20. "Reply 'keep' to stay on the list, anything else and I'll stop reaching out."
21. "If this isn't your priority right now, who is the right person?"

## Question-as-ask (works when audience needs a softer entry)

22. "Is <pain> on your roadmap this half?"
23. "Are you running <motion> with <constraint>?"
24. "Did <signal> change anything on your side?"
25. "Have you decided between <A> and <B> yet?"

## Soft binary asks (re-engagement)

26. "Anything change since we last chatted?"
27. "Still relevant?"
28. "Worth picking this back up?"
29. "Should I close the loop here, or follow up next quarter?"
30. "Is this still on the radar?"

## What makes these work

- **Yes or no answerable**. No "share your thoughts."
- **Time-bound where possible**. "Thursday at 11am PT" beats "sometime
  next week."
- **One specific ask**. Not stacked.
- **Low cost to reply**. The recipient should be able to answer in
  ≤5 words.

## What kills binary CTAs

- Trailing softeners: "Worth 15 minutes Thursday — but no pressure, of
  course, totally understand if not." Cut everything after the question.
- Multiple options: "Could do Thursday or Friday or Monday next week,
  or whenever else works for you." Pick one specific time.
- Hedging language: "Would it possibly be of interest..." Just ask.
- Stacking: "Worth a chat? Or should I send the doc? Or both?" Pick one.

## When to break the binary rule

Almost never. The rare exception: **first-line introductions to
referrals**. There, an open-ended question can work because the
recipient is pre-warmed by the referrer.

But in cold outreach, binary always wins.
